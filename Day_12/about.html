// origin story

Born from a dumpster in 1998

VintageVault began when our founder, James Corrigan, pulled a fully intact PDP-8 minicomputer from a skip behind a demolished university building in Edinburgh. He spent six months restoring it, documented everything, and listed it on a Usenet group.

Twenty-seven years later, we have authenticated and rehomed over 4,000 computing artefacts — from punch card decks to prototype workstations — to museums, universities, and private collectors across 60 countries.

We believe these machines are not just objects. They are compressed history — physical records of how humanity learned to think with electricity.

"Every transistor was a decision.
Every line of code was an argument.
These machines are primary sources."

— James Corrigan, Founder

// how we work

The Authentication Process

01

Acquisition

We source items from university disposals, estate sales, lab clearances, and a global network of trusted sellers. We inspect every item in person before purchase.

02

Authentication

Serial numbers, PCB revisions, and manufacturing stamps are cross-referenced against our internal database and external archives. Provenance documents are digitised and archived.

03

Assessment

Each item receives a condition grade (Museum Quality → Display Only). Electronics are bench-tested; capacitors, batteries, and belts replaced only where necessary and documented.

04

Documentation

Every item gets a full photographic record, condition report, provenance summary, and a technical specification sheet before listing in our catalog.

05

Conservation

Items are stored in climate-controlled conditions with humidity control. We never over-restore — patina and original wear are part of the historical record.

06

Rehoming

Each item ships with custom wooden crating, full documentation, and a Certificate of Authenticity. We provide lifetime consultation for every purchase.

Milestones

1998

The First Find

James recovers a PDP-8 from a skip in Edinburgh. Lists it on Usenet. Ships to a buyer in California for $200.

2002

VintageVault.co.uk launches

First dedicated website. 40 items listed. Martha Liu joins as the first restoration specialist.

2008

Museum Partnership

First institutional contract — sourcing for the Computer History Museum in Mountain View, CA.

2015

1,000th Item Rehomed

An original Altair 8800 with documented provenance to the MITS factory ships to a private collector in Tokyo.

2021

Climate-Controlled Archive

New dedicated facility in Bristol with 2,000 sq ft of climate-controlled storage opens.
